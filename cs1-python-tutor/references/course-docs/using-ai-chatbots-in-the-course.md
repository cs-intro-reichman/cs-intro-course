# Using AI chatbots in the course

Using AI Chatbots: A Guide to the Perplexed
שימוש בסוכני בינה מלאכותית: מורה נבוכים ונבוכות
By “AI Chatbots” we refer to such programs as ChatGPT (by OpenAI), Claude (by Anthropic), Copilot (by Microsoft), and similar tools. An “AI chatbot” can be described as a conversational search engine that simulates an intelligent, personal assistant.
Chatbots can greatly enhance your learning experience. Using a chatbot is optional. If you use one, we recommend the course AI tutor, described in the next section, and not other chatbots. If you choose to use chatbots, it’s important to do so wisely and responsibly. Over-reliance on chatbots can diminish your ability to think independently and use information critically. Specifically in your journey as a computer science student, becoming too dependent on chatbots can inhibit your performance in two mission-critical situations: exams and job interviews. In both situations, you won’t have access to chatbots or to the Internet. Therefore, to succeed in this course and beyond, it’s essential to develop skills to solve problems and write code independently.
So, how can you use chatbots wisely, to enhance your learning performance and career prospects? That’s what this little document is all about.
The course AI tutor (recommended)
The course has its own AI tutor. It runs inside Claude and was set up by the course staff for this course. It knows the lecture slides, the code examples and the homework assignments, and it follows the guidelines in this document: it explains and guides, but it doesn’t solve homework for you. We recommend that you use it as your AI chatbot in this course.
Why the course tutor and not another chatbot? Other chatbots are not set up for this course, and we can’t control what they do. They don’t know our lectures, they often use Python that the course hasn’t taught yet, and when asked, they usually hand you a complete homework solution, which takes away the practice you need for the exam. The course tutor knows where you are in the course: it uses only the Python taught so far, points you to the relevant lecture slide, and guides you through homework one step at a time. For these reasons, we don’t recommend using other chatbots for this course.
Installing the tutor. The tutor, with step-by-step instructions for installing, using and updating it, is at github.com/cs-intro-reichman/cs-intro-course. Installing takes about two minutes. After that, open a new chat in Claude and ask your question: the tutor switches on by itself, and you don’t need to mention it.
Using the tutor. Ask it the way you would ask a teaching assistant, in English or in Hebrew. For example:
•	“I don’t understand what return does. Can you explain it with an example from the lecture?”
•	“My program prints 0 instead of 3. Here is my code: …” The tutor shows you where the problem is and helps you find the fix yourself.
•	“I’m stuck on Homework 1, program 3. Here is what I tried: …” Always show what you tried. The tutor answers with hints and leading questions, one step at a time.
•	“Give me three practice questions on loops for the exam.”
The tutor won’t write homework solutions for you, even if you ask. It will offer a hint instead. This is on purpose: you’ll need to solve such problems on your own in the exam.
Keeping it up to date. New lectures and homework assignments are added to the tutor during the semester. When the course staff announce a new version, or when the tutor tells you that a topic isn’t in its material yet, reinstall it as explained on the tutor’s page (github.com/cs-intro-reichman/cs-intro-course).
1. Use AI for research and clarification, not shortcuts
Use chatbots for answering questions and providing explanations. Don’t use them as a shortcut to avoid doing the hard work yourself. For example, if you are working on some programming or algorithmic problem, ask the chatbot to explain the underlying principles, not to solve the problem for you. Understanding the why and how is far more important than getting to the right answer. For example, suppose that Bob and Alice were asked to write a program that performs binary search. Bob prompted the chatbot to “Write a Python program that performs binary search”. Alice’s prompt was “Explain binary search, and give an example”. Bob will undermine his ability to write code without external help. Alice will be motivated and guided to solve the problem on her own.
2. Verify information
AI chatbots can provide incorrect or incomplete information. Therefore, develop a habit to double-check what the chatbot says. To do so, confirm the chatbot’s answers using your course materials and common sense. For example, after asking "How can I check if two strings are equal in Python?", a chatbot answered: ”You can compare the two strings using the is operator”. As it turns out, this answer sometimes works, but is generally incorrect (the right operator is ==). The course AI tutor makes mistakes like this less often, because it works from the course material, but it can still be wrong, so check its answers too.
3. Ask clear and specific questions
For example, instead of asking "What is a loop?" ask "Can you explain the difference between a for loop and a while loop?" This will lead to more detailed and useful explanations, helping you gain deeper insights into the material.
4. Don't rely on AI for problem-solving
When it comes to programming or solving algorithmic problems, it's important to practice handling these problems on your own. Deep learning happens only when you struggle with a problem, make mistakes, and debug your code by trial and error. To reiterate an important point: In your exams and job interviews, you will not have access to AI assistance. Therefore, you must develop your ability to approach and solve problems on your own.
5. Use AI to supplement, not replace, your learning
Chatbots can supplement your learning experience, but they don’t replace traditional learning methods. Attending lectures, and doing homework assignments on your own, are vital components of a strong computer science foundation. Use chatbots as an additional resource to clarify doubts and fill in missing information, but keep your primary focus on learning through direct engagement with the course materials. In particular, go over the lecture slides after each lecture, play with the given code examples, and, optionally, use a chatbot to enhance your learning. For example, you can copy-paste a code example that was given in the lecture, and ask the chatbot to explain, or improve it. The course AI tutor already knows the lecture slides, so you can simply ask, for example, “Explain the code on slide 16 of lecture 4-1”.
6. Learn to think critically and creatively
One of the key skills you will develop in computer science is critical thinking. While chatbots are great at providing answers, they don’t teach you how to ask the right questions or think creatively. Push yourself to explore multiple solutions to a problem and consider alternative approaches. This kind of thinking will serve you well not only in exams but also in your future career as a computer scientist. For example, if you wrote code that solves a problem, and the code works correctly, you can copy-paste the code into the chatbot, and ask it to show you other ways to solve the problem. Seeing multiple solutions and understanding their pros and cons provides a tremendous educational value. (For a homework assignment, the course AI tutor will review your code and discuss other approaches with you, but it won’t write them out, because the assignment is graded.)
7. Use old school learning techniques
When you sit for job interviews, recruiters will assess your problem-solving skills and knowledge, not your ability to use AI tools. Focus on understanding the core concepts thoroughly so that you can demonstrate your skills confidently without external assistance. Practicing problems by hand, using paper and pencil, writing on whiteboards, coding from scratch in pseudo-code: these are the best techniques for preparing for exams and job interviews.
